## Prose, links and inline semantics

A paragraph of ordinary Article prose, long enough to wrap at least twice inside the 768 px lined rail so the measure can be judged against a real column rather than against a single line. It carries an [internal link](/blog/streaming-with-suspense), an [external link](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/directives/use-cache), a same-Article link to [the tables section](#tables), **strong text**, _emphasis_, ~~struck-through text~~ and `inlineCode` so the inline vocabulary sits in one place.

Press <Kbd>⌘</Kbd> <Kbd>K</Kbd> to open the command menu. Inline `Kbd` has to sit on the prose baseline without pushing the line box, which is the whole reason it is worth looking at inside a paragraph rather than on its own.

## Long content

<Callout kind="danger" title="A Callout nobody should write">
This Callout runs for several lines on purpose. Its job is to show what a filled
surface does when it stops being an aside and starts being a section: whether the
inset ring still reads at this height, whether the icon column looks abandoned
next to a block of text, and whether the reader's eye can still find the prose
that follows it. It carries a [link](https://nextjs.org/docs), some `inline

The second paragraph exists because a Callout's internal rhythm is not the prose column's rhythm, and the difference only shows up when there is more than one block inside it.
